What is a Child Travel Consent Letter?

A child travel consent letter is a signed document from a parent or legal guardian giving permission for a minor to travel internationally — with one parent, a relative, or another adult. It helps prevent international child abduction and may be requested by border officers, airlines, or immigration officials.

What Should Be Included?

  • Consenting parent(s) full name(s)
  • Child's full name and date of birth
  • Accompanying person's name and relationship
  • Destination country or countries
  • Travel dates (departure and return)
  • Purpose of travel
  • Emergency contact information
  • Parent(s) signature(s)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a consent letter required for a child to cross the US-Canada border?
While not legally mandatory, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) and US Customs strongly recommend a consent letter when a child is travelling with only one parent or with someone who is not their parent or guardian. Border officers can deny entry without one.
Does the letter need to be notarized?
CBSA does not require notarization, but it is strongly recommended for international travel outside North America. A notarized letter is taken more seriously by foreign border officials. For US travel, a signed letter is generally sufficient.
What if one parent is deceased or has sole custody?
If one parent is deceased, bring a copy of the death certificate. If you have sole custody, bring a copy of the custody agreement or court order. Include a reference to these documents in the consent letter for clarity.
Should both parents sign the letter?
Yes — ideally both parents or guardians should sign. If only one parent is signing, note in the letter why the other parent is not signing (e.g. sole guardian, other parent deceased, other parent is the accompanying adult).
Can this letter be used if the child is travelling alone?
This template is designed for travel with an adult companion. If your child is travelling as an unaccompanied minor, contact the airline directly — they have specific unaccompanied minor programs and forms that apply.

Who Needs a Child Travel Consent Letter in Canada?

The Government of Canada recommends that any child travelling outside Canada without both parents or legal guardians carry a signed consent letter. Here are the most common situations where you need one:

Child Travelling with One Parent

If you are travelling alone with your child — even if you are married — the non-travelling parent should sign a consent letter. Canadian border officers and airline staff may ask for it, especially when crossing into the United States.

Child Travelling to the USA

Canada–US border crossings are the most common situation where a travel consent letter is requested. US Customs and Border Protection may ask for written proof that both parents have authorized the trip, even for short visits or day trips.

Child Travelling with Grandparents or Relatives

When a child travels with grandparents, aunts, uncles, or family friends, both parents must sign the consent letter. This applies to international travel as well as cruises departing from Canadian ports.

School Trips and Group Travel

Many school boards and trip organizers require a signed travel consent letter before allowing a child to participate in international school trips or sports tournaments abroad.

Does the Letter Need to Be Notarized?

The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) does not legally require notarization, but strongly recommends it for international travel outside North America. A notarized letter is taken more seriously by foreign border officials and reduces the chance of delays. For travel to the USA, a signed letter is generally sufficient.
