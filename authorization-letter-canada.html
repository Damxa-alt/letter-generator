What is an Authorization Letter?

An authorization letter is a signed document that grants another person permission to act on your behalf for a specific purpose and time period. It is commonly used when you cannot be physically present to complete a task or transaction yourself.

What Should Be Included?

  • Your full legal name
  • The authorized person's full name
  • Their relationship to you (if relevant)
  • A clear description of what they are authorized to do
  • The start and end date of authorization
  • Your signature and contact information

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an authorization letter need to be notarized in Canada?
In most everyday situations — picking up documents, Canada Post, government offices — notarization is not required. A signed letter is sufficient. However, for financial, legal, or real estate matters, some institutions may require notarization. Always confirm with the receiving party beforehand.
Can I use this letter at Canada Post to pick up a package?
Yes. Canada Post accepts a signed authorization letter along with a valid photo ID from the authorized person. Make sure the letter clearly states the purpose and includes your name and contact information.
Is this letter valid for banking purposes in Canada?
An authorization letter can support banking requests, but most major Canadian banks have their own power of attorney or authorized signing forms. Use this letter as a starting point and confirm with your bank what additional documentation they require.
How long is an authorization letter valid?
The letter is valid for the date range you specify. There is no legal maximum in Canada, but it is best practice to limit the authorization to the shortest time necessary for the task.
Can I authorize more than one person?
This generator creates a letter for one authorized person. If you need to authorize multiple people, you can generate separate letters for each individual.
