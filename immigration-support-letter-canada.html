What is an Immigration Support Letter?

An immigration support letter is a written statement from a Canadian citizen or permanent resident confirming their support for an applicant's immigration or visa application. It can demonstrate financial support, moral support, or family ties. IRCC and visa officers use these letters to assess the applicant's connections to Canada and their likelihood of returning home after a visit.

What Should Be Included?

  • Supporter's full name and immigration status
  • Supporter's address and contact information
  • Applicant's full name and relationship
  • Type of application being supported
  • Nature of support (financial, moral, sponsorship)
  • Purpose of the visit or immigration
  • Supporter's signature and date

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an immigration support letter required for a Canadian visitor visa?
While not always mandatory, an immigration support letter significantly strengthens a visitor visa (TRV) application. It helps demonstrate that the applicant has genuine ties to Canada and a host who will support them during their stay. IRCC officers consider it a positive supporting document.
Who can write an immigration support letter in Canada?
The support letter should be written by a Canadian citizen or permanent resident who has a genuine relationship with the applicant — such as a spouse, parent, sibling, relative, or close friend living in Canada. The writer's immigration status and address in Canada are important details to include.
What is the difference between an invitation letter and a support letter?
An invitation letter invites someone to visit Canada for a specific purpose and period. A support letter goes further by confirming the writer's commitment to supporting the applicant financially or morally throughout their immigration process. Both can be submitted together to strengthen an application.
Can a support letter help with a PR or sponsorship application?
Yes. For family sponsorship applications under IRCC's Family Class, a support letter from the Canadian sponsor confirms their commitment to financially supporting the applicant. It is typically submitted alongside the official sponsorship forms and financial documents.
Does the support letter need to be notarized?
Notarization is generally not required for immigration support letters submitted to IRCC. However, the letter should be signed, dated, and include the supporter's contact information. For certain legal or humanitarian applications, additional authentication may be required.
